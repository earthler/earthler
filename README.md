<h1 align="center">Earthler</h1>

<p align="center">
  <strong>A digital ecosystem for autonomous communities.</strong><br>
  Those Who Steward Their World.
</p>

> **In early development.** There is no version to install yet.

An Earthler is someone who takes care of their own world and takes responsibility for it, anywhere: in an ecovillage, on a rural property or in the middle of a city.

## What we want to build

An internet a community installs on its own machine. A community of up to a thousand people downloads it, installs it and reaches it through the browser. Once configured, it should keep working even if the connection to the world ends.

- **Talk.** Residents will be able to talk, make calls and receive emergency alerts.
- **Learn.** They will be able to browse Wikipedia, manuals, maps and content about their own region, publish lessons on what they know and watch public domain films.
- **Decide.** They will be able to mark on the local map what needs solving and decide together what is shared, voting directly or lending their vote to someone they trust.
- **Ask.** The Mentor will be the voice of that place's knowledge, a local artificial intelligence, the same for everyone and most useful to those who don't know the subject.

## What we believe

Technology serves life. Life belongs with nature. A society can be highly technological and live in the middle of the woods, with gardens, livestock and buildings. We seek balance between nature, human beings and technology, and each one needs to hold its place: humanity as purpose, technology as instrument, nature as foundation.

You don't have to live off-grid to be an Earthler. You have to think beyond the grid.

The full text is in the [manifesto](MANIFESTO.md).

## What is here

The first pieces of the system: the journal that everything is derived from, the indexes rebuilt from it, and a first screen where residents write in an open channel and find it through search. It runs over plain HTTP, has no passphrase and protects nothing personal yet, so it must not be used to run a community.

[`MAP.md`](MAP.md) says where everything is, and [`docs/design`](docs/design) says why each part is the way it is.

[`experiments/`](experiments) holds small, disposable programs that test the riskiest parts of the design on real devices before the system is built on top of them. The first one is the [access proof](experiments/access-proof).
